Real estate CRM

A multi-tenant CRM for realtors, built around a live building view where every flat shows its status, with leads matched to flats automatically.

The brief

What needed solving

Realtors juggle inventory across projects and towers, buyers with different budgets, and deals that tie up specific flats. General-purpose CRMs don't understand any of that, so teams end up back in spreadsheets.

What I built

The solution

  • A building view that draws each tower floor by floor, with every flat coloured by status: vacant, for sale, for rent, on hold, booked, sold or rented
  • One-click actions on any flat: change its status, hold it for a client with a countdown, book a site visit or start a deal
  • Matching both ways: flats that fit a client's budget, size and purpose, and clients who fit a flat
  • A full sales toolkit: lead scoring, a drag-and-drop pipeline, tasks, calendar, email templates, and reports with CSV and PDF export
  • A visual automation builder with triggers, conditions, actions and delays
  • Subscription billing with trials, plan changes and GST invoices, plus a separate console to run the platform
Under the hood

How it's built

  • Each workspace's data is isolated at the database level with PostgreSQL row-level security
  • Holds release themselves when the timer ends, and winning a deal marks the flat sold or rented automatically
  • The platform console is a separate app with its own login, mandatory two-factor sign-in and a full audit log
  • Billing works with Stripe or Razorpay through one shared payment state machine
  • Plans, prices, limits and feature flags are all data, so they change without a deploy
Outcome

Where it landed

Realtors see their whole inventory at a glance and act on it in a click, and the business side of the product (plans, billing, feature flags) runs from an admin console instead of code changes.
